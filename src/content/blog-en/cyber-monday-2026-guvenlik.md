## Technical view

The FTC's November 19, 2026 alert says scammers place paid search ads next to legitimate companies' ads, sometimes at the very top. A hurried click can lead to a fake site where the victim pays a scammer or shares information such as a bank account number. The three concrete steps are: scroll below the paid results, search the seller name together with "complaint" or "scam", and type the address directly into the browser if you know it.

Brand impersonation is not limited to search engines. The FTC's November 2, 2026 record describes scammers impersonating well-known businesses and government agencies in ads on social media, search results, and shopping platforms. The agency is considering updates to the existing Impersonation Rule or a new rule and is accepting comments until November 30, 2026. Saying the FTC has already issued a new rule would be wrong; the review is still open.

Payment is the sharpest decision point. The FTC says a seller who insists on gift cards, wire transfers, cryptocurrency, or a payment app is likely a scammer, while a credit card offers dispute rights and a way to withhold payment during an investigation. Amazon's guidance, updated November 20, 2026, says payment happens only in its app, on its website, or in a physical store; payment or bank transfer requested by phone or email is not legitimate. Artificial urgency such as "your account will be closed" and pressure to buy gift cards should be rejected outright.

The second trap is the fake CAPTCHA. In its June 8, 2026 warning, the FTC describes pages that look like ordinary human verification but ask the user to press Windows+R, paste a command with Ctrl+V, and hit Enter, silently installing malware. Real CAPTCHAs do not ask for command execution. If that sequence was followed, disconnect the device, run a security scan, and change passwords and two-factor authentication from another device.

The date itself is also easy to overstate. Amazon's FAQ gives November 30 for Cyber Monday while starting its own event on November 19. The sources show a single day surrounded by longer sale windows. "What is Cyber Monday" is therefore not a strict 24-hour event; it is a retail label anchored to the Monday after US Thanksgiving and stretched by individual retailers.

## What to do

1. **Navigate directly.** Type the retailer's known address or open its official app instead of clicking a search result.
2. **Verify the seller separately.** Search the store name plus "complaint" or "scam"; a logo and polished layout are not identity checks.
3. **Keep the payment method.** Use a credit card; stop if the seller asks for a gift card, cryptocurrency, a wire transfer, or a bank account from a message.
4. **Verify messages inside the channel.** Do not use links in an order or refund email/SMS; check the account in the retailer's own app.
5. **Leave command-prompt CAPTCHAs.** Real verification never asks for executable commands; if you ran them, isolate the device immediately.
6. **Keep evidence and report.** Save the order confirmation, screenshots, and offer terms, then report the ad to the FTC and the platform.

## What not to claim

- Do not say the top result is safe. The FTC describes that position as an opportunity for scammers.
- Do not say HTTPS proves the store is real. HTTPS encrypts transport; it does not verify the seller's identity.
- Do not say discounts exist only on November 30. Amazon started on November 19 and other retailers set their own windows.
- Do not call gift-card payment fast and normal. The FTC lists it as a scam indicator.
- Do not claim real CAPTCHAs sometimes ask for commands. The FTC says the opposite.
- Do not say the FTC approved a new rule. The November 2 record describes an open review.
- Do not call this a CVE or software flaw. These records concern consumer fraud and platform abuse.

## Status

As of November 24, 2026, the date and security guidance are verified from Amazon's FAQ and FTC alerts published on November 2, November 19, June 8, and July 22. Verified facts: Cyber Monday is November 30, 2026; Amazon's event runs November 19-30; paid ads can outrank or sit beside legitimate results; the FTC's three seller checks; payment-method warnings; the fake CAPTCHA command flow; and the fact that the FTC's rule review remains open. Open questions: Turkish retailer dates are not covered by these sources; no 2026 spend estimate is adopted here; and nothing in the records says organic results are inherently safe. Rising search interest does not by itself prove a rise in fraud volume; it only shows that users are asking these questions.
