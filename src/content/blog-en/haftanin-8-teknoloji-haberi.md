Two narratives ran in parallel this week: **security shocks**—user keys exposed in a popular AI coding tool, and the ad ecosystem documented as a path to zero-click spyware—and **open-source momentum**—Meta opened an 816B model, DeepSeek released its production inference engine, Mistral entered time-series models, and Linux shipped a new release.

## 1. OpenCode leak: “rotate your keys”

Unauthorized access to a legacy system may have exposed third-party provider keys (e.g., OpenAI/Anthropic) stored in workspaces. Action: revoke/rotate keys, audit usage, update clients.

## 2. Meta Muse Spark Open (816B)

A major open-weight release signaling continued competition at scale and renewed focus on evals, licensing, and deployment costs.

## 3. DeepSeek DeepInfer

Open-sourcing inference infrastructure highlights that performance and serving economics are as strategic as model weights.

## 4. Citizen Lab: AdFection

Research connecting ad delivery pipelines to surveillance/spyware risk reinforces that the ad supply chain is part of the security perimeter.

## 5. Linux 7.3

Kernel updates bring the usual mix of drivers, filesystems, and performance/security work—worth tracking for fleet stability.

## 6. Mistral time-series move

Specialized models for forecasting/time-series expand “foundation model” use beyond text.

## 7. Mercor benchmark mapping

Another attempt to make model comparisons more navigable amid benchmark sprawl and gaming concerns.

## 8. Ecosystem signals

Funding, governance, and tooling shifts this week all point the same way: AI capability, security posture, and open infrastructure are now tightly coupled.
