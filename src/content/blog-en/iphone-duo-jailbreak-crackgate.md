Friday, October 23, 2026 was launch day for Apple’s first foldable, the iPhone Duo. Two kinds of “cracks” made headlines. MacRumors published photos of screens cracking along the hinge (“Crackgate?”). Separately, the Calif team shared videos claiming a jailbreak on an iPhone Duo running iOS 27.1.

Same word, different problems. One is first-gen hardware risk; the other is a day-one challenge to Apple’s memory-safety and platform security story.

## What Calif showed

Videos showed UI effects and customization consistent with jailbreak-level access. Calif had previously demonstrated work on iPhone 18 Pro/iOS 27.0.1 and claimed persistence across reboots (untethered) in some contexts—an eyebrow-raising claim given how hardened modern iOS is.

Importantly, there was no public end-user jailbreak tool released. Researchers indicated some issues were reported to Apple and others might be; a PoC could appear, but “download jailbreak” searches are likely to find scams or malware.

## The hardware side

Hinge and display durability on first-gen foldables is a known concern. Reports and teardowns (e.g., iFixit analysis) matter for buyers—but they’re orthogonal to software exploitability.

## Practical takeaway

- Don’t install “jailbreak” tools from untrusted sources.
- Keep devices updated; vendor patches follow credible exploit chains.
- Treat day-one demos as research signals, not consumer-ready features.
