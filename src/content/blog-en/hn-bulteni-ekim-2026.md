This month Hacker News saw **29,765** submissions; the top score went to news of Margaret Hamilton’s passing (2,123 points). But the month’s real story was different: **four major model launches in one week**, a price war, a polarized passkeys debate, Cloudflare acquiring Deno and winding down the runtime’s trajectory, and the Pentagon acknowledging overreliance on AI in a school-attack context.

## AI: four launches, one week

### Claude Opus 5.5
Anthropic’s release focused on clearer, less bloated writing—addressing a common critique of prior versions.

### GPT-6 Sol & Luna (and 6.1 follow-up)
OpenAI pushed rapid iteration and pricing pressure, keeping the frontier race intense.

### Gemini 4 Argon and Mistral Large 4
Google and Mistral added to the deluge, reinforcing that model choice is now also a cost/latency/product decision.

## Security and identity
Passkeys debates split the community between phishing resistance/usability gains and recovery/lock-in concerns.

## Platforms and runtimes
Cloudflare–Deno news raised questions about stewardship of developer platforms and long-term runtime bets.

## Policy and accountability
Government/defense use of AI drew scrutiny, emphasizing human oversight where stakes are life-and-death.

## What to watch
Benchmark churn, inference costs, and security incidents around AI tooling will keep intersecting—plan for all three.
